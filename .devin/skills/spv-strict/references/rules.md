# rules — `spv/*`, strict

Each rule: the violation, the doctrine it breaks (D-numbers in
doctrine.md), how to detect it, default severity, and the fix seam in
this repo. Severity rises one step when the path moves money or durable
state; it never drops below MED.

| `spv/block-wait`                 | A deal-path step sleeps, polls or defers until a block, when proven stock or carried ancestry would have served. ## Reading the result

- A `spv/block-wait` finding always names its cause as one of: missing
  stock, missing carried evidence, or a deliberate value-depth rule. The
  third is not a finding.
- Zeroconf findings split the same way: `spv/zeroconf-unused` is a wait
  on our own verified ancestry (first-party — maximal safe zeroconf);
  `spv/thin-ancestry` is an accept that skipped the recursion;
  `spv/zeroconf-overreach` is a counterparty chain past its policy
  bound. Removing a wait by weakening a check converts a latency
  finding into a trust finding. That is never a fix.
- A lane that depends on a third-party codec's stricter rule (the
  vendored host modules) is fixed by stocking, not by forking the rule.
